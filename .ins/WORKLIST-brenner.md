## OPEN

1. **Trace width vs current on my boards** (user, relaying bronner, 2026-10-01).
   bronner's finding was "LED strip: 5 V on 103 mm of 0.25 mm track" -- that board
   (`led_strip`) is deleted now, but `fret_led_key`, `fret_led_mid` and `foot_led` deserve
   the same look and have not had it. For each board: list every power net, its worst-case
   current, the narrowest routed segment and its length, and compare against the
   IPC-2221 width for 1 oz outer copper at a 10 C rise. The nets that matter:
   * `fret_led_key`: +24 V in from J1 through F1 to the buck (0.89 A); +14 V out of the
     buck (1.38 A) along the board and into the two seam pogos; GND return.
   * `fret_led_mid`: +14 V in from the seam pogos (mid's share of the 1.38 A), GND.
   * `foot_led`: +24 V in at J1 (0.73 A through the -X board), the pass-through to J2
     (0.37 A), the 11 V rail off the buck.
   If the router's net classes do not set a power width, that is the fix, not hand edits.

2. **Foot strip board-to-board connection** (user, 2026-10-01: "we need a plan for how to
   connect the two feet led strips"). The boards carry J2 -> J1 SH sockets 24 mm mouth to
   mouth, and nothing says what cable goes between them: no stock double-ended SH cable is
   that short, and SH is not a hand-crimp family. PLAN PROPOSED, awaiting the user:
   move the sockets so the mouths are a stock cable apart (50 mm Qwiic-type 4-way SH,
   bought assembled), join the two boards at the channel mouth, slide them in as a train.
   Open questions recorded with the proposal: pin order vs the Qwiic convention; that a
   Qwiic cable invites a 3.3 V accessory onto a 24 V pin; whether pulling the -X board
   out by the cable is acceptable for service.

3. **Fret-board lip seat needs a printed coupon.** The tilt-in lip carries the board
   0.10 low nominal; the board's outline tolerance and the print's go straight into it.

4. **Seam pogo unknowns** (C5203987): spring rate is not in the datasheet (force at
   working height is), lateral registration between the two panels is taken on trust,
   stock was 602 pcs = 50 instruments, and JLC lists it as a high-difficulty assembly part.

5. **Far-end ear screw: sweep the panel's install stroke.** The ear and its button head
   were intersected with the build at rest (clear). The deck panel's own install path
   over the chassis has not been swept with the ear on it.
